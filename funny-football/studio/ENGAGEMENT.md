# Engagement engine: make people WATCH, SHARE and ARGUE
User request (Nov 15 2026): "make videos people find attractive, want to watch, want to share, want to argue about".
Run this **before writing every script**. A script must score at least 14/20 on the scorecard (section 4) to go into production.

## 1. WATCH: keep them until the end
- **Open loop in the first 3 s:** pose a question the video only answers at the end ("WHO WINS?", "ONLY 15 TO GO?!", "NO RONALDO, NO PROBLEM?"). The loop must be closed late, ideally in the last 20%.
- **Layered hook at frame 0:** a big recognisable face (portrait), the hook text and a loud sound all at once.
- **A pattern interrupt every 3–5 s:** a new round or beat, a zoom, a cut-in, a sound change or a slam. Never 5 s with nothing new (a 2-second rule is not supported by the research; 3–5 s is).
- **Escalation:** each beat is bigger than the last (Ep9: shrug → panic → fight → white flag).
- **Payoff + loop:** the twist at the end recontextualises the opening, and the last frame cuts cleanly back to the first. 20–35 s for a viral push.

## 2. SHARE: give them a reason to send it (STEPPS, Jonah Berger)
| Lever | What it means for us | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Social currency** | sharing makes the sharer look funny or "in the know" | a perfectly timed roast of a fresh result; an insider stat few people know |
| **Triggers** | tie the video to something that recurs, so it comes back to mind | "every time Ronaldo scores, the counter moves" (Road to 1000 series); every Clásico |
| **Emotion (high arousal)** | laughter, awe, playful outrage, surprise. Not sadness or calm. | the 1-4 humiliation, the SIUUU shockwave |
| **Public** | a visible signature people copy | the hidden 🐐 hunt, the goat mascot, Team 7 / Team 10 |
| **Practical value** | ammo for real arguments | the clean stat table in the caption, "save this for your next GOAT debate" |
| **Stories** | the facts ride inside a mini-story | how it started → how it's going; a fight with rounds |

The **share trigger line** goes in the caption: "Send this to a Messi fan", "Tag the friend who said Portugal don't need him" (NOT on Facebook, where it counts as bait).

## 3. ARGUE: make them want to comment (identity research)
- **Identity on the line:** people comment most when a result confirms their side or lets them mock the rival. Every episode needs **two camps** (Team 7 vs 10, Portugal fans vs Ronaldo fans, Barça vs Madrid, old GOATs vs new kids).
- **Fair but unfinished:** present the true facts so that **both sides can claim victory** (Ep7/Ep10 3-3), or leave the verdict open ("You decide"). Never call the winner ourselves.
- **Spicy but true:** frame one real stat provocatively ("146 vs 125, and it's not close"). Never invent or distort a fact.
- **"You forgot…" bait (honest version):** say on screen "We left one big stat out 👀 Which one?". Fans will flood in with their favourite stat.
- **Ranking and number questions:** "Which round was the knockout? 1–6", "Rate the SIUUUs 1–4". A number is the easiest comment to leave.
- **Pin a counter-take:** the pinned comment takes the *other* side from the caption to start a thread. Reply in the first hour to keep both camps arguing.
- **Keep it playful:** no insults to people or nations, no family, injuries or tragedies, no derogatory labels. Rivalry, not hate. Hold "potentially inappropriate" comments on YouTube.

## 6. Anti-monotony rule (user, Nov 15 2026: "isn't it too monotonous?")
Repeating beats every 3–5 s is not enough: **the beats themselves must differ.** In any series of similar segments (rounds, goals, exhibits, attempts):
- **Rotate the stage:** location, palette and lighting change each segment.
- **Rotate the gimmick:** combo counter, anime special-move strip, slow-mo, number counter, camera whip/reveal, full-screen cut-in, split screen, freeze frame.
- **Rotate the reaction layout:** both faces, loser only, winner only, full-screen, none.
- **Vary the lengths:** mix quick and longer segments (e.g. 3.4–4.6 s) and put the biggest one last.
- **Escalate:** the final segment is the biggest spectacle.
Add a line to the scorecard: **11. Do no two consecutive segments share a stage, gimmick AND reaction layout? (0–2)**. The total becomes /22; ship at 15+.

## 7. Format-level variety (user, Nov 15 2026: "still monotonous, be more creative")
Rotating backgrounds and gimmicks inside ONE format is still monotonous; the viewer sees the same template. For list-type content (comparisons, records, attempts), **change the genre per segment**: fighting game, kart race, platform jump, space shooter, pinball, quiz show, boss fight, cooking show, nature documentary, news bulletin, horror trailer…
- **Each genre brings its own camera, UI, physics, palette and sound world**, so every segment feels like a new video.
- **Keep one thread** (a running score pill, the same two characters, the mascot) so the story still connects.
- Ask before scripting: **"Could a viewer predict the next segment's look?"** If yes, change the genre.

## 8. Drama toolkit (learned on Ep11, Nov 15 2026)
- **A live race counter with lead changes and ties** (e.g. a Ballon d'Or race 1-1 → 4-1 → 5-5 😱) creates real suspense even in a timeline. Tease the tie in the hook ("wait for 2017 😱").
- **Build-ups before the biggest beats:** heartbeat, drumroll, blackout, then an explosion of rays and confetti.
- **The rival always reacts:** jealousy escalation (🙄 → 😤 → steam), a deadpan signature prop (Messi's mate), both staring at each other across the split.
- **Physical collision gags** when both lives cross (suitcases BONK at the divider).
- **End on an open loop:** a "TO BE CONTINUED ➡️" freeze makes people follow for part 2.

## 9. Creative-first, proactive (user, Nov 15 2026: "think creatively, why so simple? don't wait for me to guide you")
A direct remake of a meme/list is NOT a video. Before scripting:
- **Write 3+ bold concepts** in different frames (personify an object, a fake genre: dating show/documentary/heist, a "what if", a POV of a non-human narrator) and pick the boldest one that still keeps the facts true.
- **Self-critique as a viewer:** "Is this just the meme in motion?" If yes, throw it away.
- **Give the story a narrator or a want** (Ep12: the trophy remembers its "exes"; the CR7 beat becomes a flirt, not an insult).
- **Every era/segment gets its own look AND sound world** (film crackle 1958, VHS 1994, samba 2002, fireworks + choir 2022, smooth jazz for the CR7 "date").
- **Hunt for the little-known true detail** that makes the joke (R9 played 0 minutes in 1994; the Cascão haircut; the bisht).
- **Decide and ship.** Don't ask the user to choose between bland options; show the bold version, keep the safe one as a backup in `review/`.

## 10. Kill the "standing template" (user, Nov 15 2026: "you keep making the same old mistake: monotonous and bland")
Ep12 v1 and v2 both failed for the same root cause, even though the backgrounds changed: **every beat = one character standing in the middle + a prop appears + a caption pill**. The viewer sees the same shot 5 times, and the joke lives in the text, not in the action.
Before rendering, check the contact sheet. If 3+ frames have the same composition (one figure centered, standing, caption above), rebuild.
What works instead (Ep12 v3):
- **Continuous action:** one camera move or one chase that carries through the whole video (side-scroller, race, escape, heist). Something is always running, falling, flying or crashing.
- **Rivals on screen TOGETHER** and physically interacting (bonk, both fall in holes, one benched while the other dives).
- **Slapstick > captions:** each fact becomes a physical gag (out on penalties = a giant glove swats him; group-stage exit = he clangs into a barrier; the same-day exit = two holes open at once). The caption only confirms it.
- **Running threads + callbacks:** a live scoreboard, a reaction box (Pelé/R9 in VIP), and a callback (the Spain runners in 2010 come back in 2026).
- **Real-but-surprising facts** are the best gags: dig for coincidences (same day, same rival twice, walked past the trophy).
- **Camera = impact (Ep12 v4):** never film the punchline from far away. Punch in (1.3–1.7×) on each peak (impact, reaction face, catch), shake the screen on every hit, then pull back to show the context. Keep the HUD (score, era, captions) fixed outside the camera. Clamp the camera so the frame edge never shows.
- **Match-recap recipe (Ep13):** find the *previous* meeting and dig for its embarrassing real detail (Kane's slip at Wembley), then build the new match as its payoff ("NO SLIP ✅", 2-3 → 3-2 mirror). Draw only the gate-approved star; everyone else = generic mini players named in text. Give the arrogant side a symbol that can visibly break (the "UNBEATEN 43" throne shatters to 0).

## 11. Read the INTENT, not the words (user, Nov 15 2026: "why do you only do exactly what I say? Make it full and varied. Remember this. Don't be so simple.")
The user gives a 1-2 line brief. That is the SEED, not the spec. Before scripting, always expand it yourself:
- **Back-story:** dig up the history between the sides (previous meetings, finals, famous moments, recurring villains/heroes). Ep13 example: Euro 2024 final (Oyarzabal), Wembley Sep 2026 (Kane slip + Oyarzabal again), Spain's World Cup, the Ballon d'Or where Kane beat Yamal.
- **Faces for the STANDOUT players only (user, Nov 16: "build just a few standout players, no need to do them all"):** draw 2–4 recognisable portraits: the hero, the rival, and at most 1–2 iconic side characters (e.g. Kane, Yamal, Oyarzabal 👻). Everyone else = a generic mini with the name on the shirt/caption. Spend the saved time on story, gags and motion, not on extra likeness work. Reuse portraits that already exist in `portrait.js` freely.
- **Show every key moment as a scene**, not as text: each goal, the red card, the assist, the save, the celebration. A moment that only appears in a caption is a missed gag.
- **Several scene types inside one video:** flashback, ceremony, lounge, tunnel, match action, close-up reaction, jumbotron, payoff. Different backgrounds, palettes and sound worlds.
- **Length follows the story:** 45–60 s is fine when every 3–5 s has a new beat (Facebook favours 45–60 s anyway).
- **Self-check before shipping:** "If the user watched this, what would they say is missing?" List 5 things a fan would expect (players, moments, history, rivalry, stats). If any is missing, add it.

## 12. Slapstick quota (user, Nov 16 2026: "our videos lack slaps and the like")
Every video needs **at least 4–5 physical-comedy hits**, spread across the timeline (not only at the end):
- **Slap / smack** with a big cartoon hand (helper `slap()` in Ep13): swoosh lines → SLAP! burst → head jolt → red handprint + stars.
- **Object hits:** the ball/rebound bonks someone, a name card flies into a face, sunglasses get smacked off, a trophy falls on a head.
- **Pratfalls:** slips, trips, fall into holes, squashed flat, flying off screen spinning.
- **Each hit is motivated by the story** (the arrogant one gets slapped back down, the scared one gets a "WAKE UP!" slap, the villain ghost gets swatted away).
- **Cartoon rules:** no blood, no real injuries, no punching, nobody hurt badly. A slap is a gag, followed by stars or a funny face. Rivalry, not hate.
- **Sound:** whoosh + crack + low thump + a crowd "ooh"/laugh for each hit.

## 13. Clarity pass (user, Nov 16 2026: "not very clear")
Before rendering, watch each beat as a stranger with the sound off:
- **Show what the character sees, BIG** (full-screen TV close-up first, then the reaction). Never put the key thing in a small inset.
- **One line of dialogue per beat** in a speech bubble (2–4 simple English words: "WATCH ME 😎", "YOUR TURN!", "HIGHER!", "AGAIN!"). Bubbles beat captions.
- **Slow the actions down:** a hit needs ~0.35 s of wind-up and a 0.5 s hold for the reaction. Use per-segment slow factors (`LEN` 4th value) instead of rushing.
- **Big readable props:** ball radius ≥30 px in mid shots; one action at a time in the frame centre.

## 14. Never repeat the same gag motion (user, Nov 16 2026: "boring")
Ep15 v1 had 13 hits that were all the same swing → BONK → lump. Repetition kills a combo.
- **A "list" of hits must be a list of DIFFERENT gags**, each with its own motion, camera angle and sound: golf swing (launch off-screen, head-first landing) · cymbal clap (squash flat, spring back) · cannon (dodge ×3 Matrix-style, then hit, splat into a wall silhouette) · pinball (juggle, volley, ricochet + score pop-ups) · finisher (shockwave, blown out of the shoes, giant "K.O.").
- **Escalate**: each gag bigger than the last; end on the biggest.
- **Cut the talk scenes hard** (mock, set-ups): 3–4 s each. Spend the time on action. Target 30–40 s.
- **Characters must MOVE**: launches, flips, squash & stretch, flying off-screen, sliding down walls. A beat where both just stand is a dead beat.

## 15. Show, don't tell + break the rules (user, Nov 16 2026: "limit dialogue, it should be understood through the video; be creative and unconventional so viewers get excited")
- **Dialogue budget: ≤3 short text bubbles per video.** Everything else is told with pictures: props, faces, gestures, icons and emoji-only bubbles (😏, 👉 0 😂, 😭👉🏆×13, a thought bubble with a face in it). Labels for real titles (EURO 2016, ×5) are fine; full sentences are not.
- **Test:** watch with the sound off and the text blurred. If the story still reads, ship it.
- **Break a convention in every video:** an impossible camera move, a genre switch mid-video, a fourth-wall break (a character grabs the combo counter, the K.O. text hits someone), a callback character from an older episode, an ending nobody expects.
- **Excitement curve:** start on a hit (frame 0 action), escalate every 3–5 s, end on the biggest moment + an open loop.

## 16. Act the hits: victims suffer, attackers gloat (user, Nov 16 2026)
- **Victim:** sobbing face (open-mouth wail + tear streams), damage that ACCUMULATES with each hit (bruise → black eye → plasters → head bandage → lumps), and a **shielding/trembling pose between hits** (hands up, shaking). Never a neutral face after a hit. Helper: `hurtFx()` + `SHAKEP(t)` in Ep15.
- **Attacker:** gleeful and menacing: evil grin, "HEHE 😈"/"HAHA", fist pumps, cracking knuckles before the next hit. Helper: `gloat()`.
- **Show damage as BRUISES, not bumps** (user, Nov 16: bumps looked wrong): `hurtFx(level 1–6)` = cheek bruise + scratches → black eye + plaster → swollen cheek → both black eyes + 2nd plaster → head bandage → chin bruise. Damage carries over into later scenes (the victim stays bandaged). No bump columns.
- **Bystanders react too** (laugh, gasp, cover eyes). A hit lands three times: the impact, the victim's reaction, the crowd's reaction.
- Sound: a sob/wail for the victim and a chuckle for the attacker after every big hit.

## 17. Comment engine v2 (user, Nov 17 2026: "nobody comments")
Sources (vendor blogs, unverified numbers): [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/increase-tiktok-comments-engagement-2026), [Satura AI](https://saturaai.com/blog/how-to-get-more-comments-youtube-shorts), [TopQLearn](https://topqlearn.com/how-to-get-more-comments-on-tiktok).
1. **Comments follow views.** First check the numbers (views, average % viewed, swipe-away). If views are low, fix the hook/cover/title first; no question will create comments from nobody.
2. **Drop the generic ask** ("comment 7 or 10" is easy to ignore). Use:
   - **The honest "you forgot…" gap:** leave real trophies out on purpose and ask "We left some of Ronaldo's trophies out 👀 which one?" (only when it is true) Fans love correcting.
   - **A controversial take from the channel** in the pinned comment ("Ronaldo would still win 1 vs 3 😤"); people reply to disagree.
   - **A hidden detail** to find (a hidden 🐐 in one frame → "found it at 0:12").
   - **Ultra-low effort:** a one-emoji or one-number answer.
3. **Seed + speed:** post the pinned comment right away, reply to every comment in the first hour, and on TikTok answer the best comment with a video reply (a free extra post).
4. Log comments per video in performance.csv so the daily run can learn what works.

## 18. Data lesson: short + true story wins (YouTube analytics, Nov 17 2026)
- **Ep2 "GOAT Dictionary" (22 s):** 3.1K views in 4 days (+1.9K above channel typical), avg view duration 0:20 = **89.2%**, retention starts above 100% (people replay). Ep14 (49 s): 1.3K views, flat after ~2 h.
- **Length (user decision, Nov 17): every episode is 30–45 s, one cut for all platforms.** Plan for ~35 s: setup ≤3 s, combo ≤3–4 gags of ~2 s, reversal by ~12–15 s, payback, K.O. Never 50 s+.
- **Ending:** the last ~1 s loses ~30% of viewers. No slow end card/outro: end on the punchline frame and cut so it loops back into the hook (last frame should visually lead into frame 1).
- **Premise:** Ep2 is a real, checkable story (club posts + real result) told as a cartoon. Real-event hooks + cartoon payoff > pure invented fights. Mix: ~1 real-event episode for every invented one.
- Slapstick/no-dialogue rules (§12–§16) still apply, compressed into the shorter runtime.
- **IG data, Ep15 (Nov 17):** skip rate 21% (better than usual) and shares 0.4% (above usual), but likes/saves/comments below usual, comments 0%. The hook and the K.O. payoff work; what is missing is a reason to comment or save. Fix: end on an open question the viewer answers in comments (a vote: "who wins round 2? 🐐 or 👑"), and keep runtime short (§18).
- **IG retention, Ep15 51 s (Nov 17):** -32% in the first 5 s (setup/mock too slow), steady loss through gags 2–4 of the 14 s combo, then flat from 25 s to the end (payback/Messi act holds everyone). Rule: setup ≤3 s, max 3 gags per combo (~2 s each), get to the reversal by ~12 s. 91% of IG views come from the Reels tab, so the first frame/1 s must work for strangers.
- **No flat edge bands (Facebook, Nov 17):** FB said "remove the border on your reel" on Ep15. Our frames had 300–580 px of flat colour (plain sky gradient at the top, plain road at the bottom), which Meta reads as letterbox borders and down-ranks. Every background must have detail touching the top and bottom 10% of the frame (clouds, sun glow, birds, curb, sidewalk, props). Check with the flat-band scan (row min/max difference <6 over >40 px = fail) before release. FB retention is also the harshest (−70% by 6 s): the first second must already show the hit/gag.
- **Sound variety (user, Nov 17):** see `studio/SOUND_BIBLE.md`. No stock WAV voices; per-character procedural voices (vox2) + per-hit re-synthesized impacts (fx2); new music style every episode.
- **Production layer (option C, Nov 18):** always render with `toon2/render_pro.sh` (60 fps → motion blur, bloom, grade, mastered audio) and call PRO.grade / PRO.impact (every shake ≥14) / PRO.whip (shot starts) / PRO.shadow in render(). Draw goals with the depth goal() from Ep19, never a flat side-on frame (user: "weird goal").
- **Pacing (user, Nov 18: "long-winded and bland"):** no shot longer than ~2.5 s unless something new happens in it; cut every second where nobody moves; endings ≤2 s (hold the punchline, then loop). Ep19 tight cut = 16.4 s from the 31 s master. The 30–45 s length rule yields to this when a cut drags.
- **Story clarity (user, Nov 18: "the story isn't clear"):** open with the WHY (the event that starts it, e.g. Ronaldo quitting), then ONE big chapter caption per beat at the top ("NEXT GAME: …", "RONALDO WATCHING: …"). Captions are narration, not speech bubbles, so they don't count toward the ≤3 bubble rule. Sound-off test: a stranger must be able to retell the story from the captions alone. Clever devices (like the DAYS WITHOUT sign) only if they don't compete with the captions.
- **Scoreboards (user, Nov 18):** use the big TV-style board (`bigBoard()` in toon2/ep19): 1000 px wide, flag-colour team blocks, 128 px digits on white, pulse + glow when the score changes, red sub-label for events (10 MEN, FULL TIME). Never show an intermediate score you can't source (e.g. show "vs" until the verified final).
- **Bring the taunt to the victim (user, Nov 18):** the payoff lands harder when the winners physically show up in the loser's space (Ep19: Portugal climb out of Ronaldo's TV and SIUUU around his sofa). Prefer face-to-face taunts over celebrating somewhere else.
